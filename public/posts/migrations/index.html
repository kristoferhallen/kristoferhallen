Zeno´s migration

28 Sep 2024

Migrations, changing technology like tools, libraries or bigger solutions, are sometimes straight forward, clearly define the starting and end point, plan and execute. But in more complex systems I see a tendency for migrations to fail when the plan is to “go from A to B” migration. System could be complex from a code or intergratin size perspective of view but even if it is a smaller technical migration complexity can be added also from organisational point of view, many people involved with different processes and different priorities. In those large system contexts we find problems like:

  • Unclear starting point A. A might not be well defined but a wide range of versions/solutions of the technology you want to migrate.
  • A moving target state B. The eco-system changes faster than you can migrate. If you are not fast enough your planned end point will be irrelevant. People have already started to look into new solutions.

That is why I liked this thread by Jean Yang, “Zeno’s migrations” migrations that start but never quite finish. She writes about developer tooling but the ideas can be applied to other migrations too. So instead of focusing on full migrations from A to B instead give up on full migrations and accept heterogeneity.

Of course there are times when the only choice is to do a full migration but it is valuable to change mindset, maybe even stop talking about migrations and instead focus on the eco-system that is acceptable to use. Instead of focus on going from A to B start accepting that there will be continous change. The “migration” could be a sliding window when a specific version slowly stops being used.
