](https://www.linkedin.com/in/johanna-axehill-61584514a/)

In the earlier articles [part #1]({{< relref "technicalleadership.md" >}}) and [part #2]({{< relref "technicalleadership2.md" >}}) we have described the need for and scope of the technical leader role. How do we then get the roles established and the technical leaders grow over time in our companies? Especially, when technical development pace is faster than ever, we as technical leaders (and our organisations!) need a structured method and valuable tools to be able to work with expectations on the technical leadership role, both today and for future challenges, and as a guidance to find this structured path towards change.

In this article, we will dig into a method on how to work with this identification of technical leadership skills and actions. The method has strong industrial grounding, due to the fact that it has been developed within the [Technical leadership program](https://expertlearninglab.se/case/technical-leadership-program/), co-created by four major Swedish engineering companies – ABB, Ericsson, Volvo Group and Saab – and directed to the needs of senior technical leaders at the cooperating organisations within this program.

## The Method for Growth and Change

The method developed within the program is based on two tools:

![The two tools: the Technical leadership framework, focusing on the purpose, skills and ways of working of the technical leader, and the Cross-hair model, focusing on the actions of the technical leader](/technicalleader3-tools.png)

The tools are to be considered as dynamic – to cover the specific needs of a specific individual or organisation. So, change, add, remove to make it valuable tools for you!

### Technical leadership framework

The Technical leadership framework describes the purpose and ways of working of a technical leader. It is also a means to formulate the expectations people have upon the technical leader, and what type of value the technical leader is expected to deliver:

* The purpose of the technical leader is to understand how to meet the needs of both business, organisation, and technology/product
* Technical leadership ways of working, capturing what skills to hone as a technical leader, and how to act as a valuable resource within the organisation

![Purpose of the technical leader: understand customers and business needs in order to contribute to a strategic vision, drive the latest technologies and develop innovative products and services, and define and maintain a clear vision so that we, as a team, work towards a common goal](/technicalleader3-purpose.png)

![The technical leadership ways of working: communicate complex problems clearly and understandable for the target audience, build bridges between different disciplines to ensure that the parts fit together as a whole, and engage, drive and motivate others to get progress, change, innovation, results and reach goals](/technicalleader3-waysofworking.png)

### Cross-hair model

![The Cross-hair model, with the axes Building self – Building others and Internal – External](/technicalleader3-crosshair.png)

The Cross-hair model describes how technical leaders act along the following dimensions:

* Building self – Building others: The technical leader must constantly invest in him/herself to stay relevant, but also develop co-workers to ensure that the organisation remains competitive.
* Internal – External: The technical leader's focus can be internal by contributing to organisation internal activities, or external by operating to obtain new knowledge outside the organisation boundaries and influencing entities.

Together, these four quadrants define the contribution areas for a technical leader. Ideally, the technical leader should make contributions in all four areas over time.

## Step 1: Identifying Current Technical Leadership Capabilities

First step for a technical leader individual (or a group) is to identify current state, with the purpose to identify if current expectations are met. This step is based on self-reflection, where the technical leader asks him/herself questions based on the Technical leadership framework and the Cross-hair model, for example:

* What are my current strengths as a technical leader?
* What stands out as most relevant or meaningful to myself in my role?
* If I were to rate myself in each of the four quadrants of the Cross-hair model, where would I place myself today?

## Step 2: Define Desired Change to Meet Future Capabilities

Based on the current state, next is then to identify the general need for change, and in what specific direction the change is needed:

* To meet the expectations on an individual acting in technical leadership role
* To meet future technology demands of the product or organisation

Again, we use the method of reflective questions directed to the technical leader him/herself, for example using questions such as:

* What characterises me as an excellent technical leader in the future?
* What is most important for me to strengthen, deepen or explore further – and why?
* Which of the characteristics for a true bridge builder do I find the most important for the future? Which of them do I need to strengthen?
* How could I build my community for the future?

When the change areas are identified, the Cross-hair model is used to identify how to implement the change, e.g., increased technical knowledge within the organisation, or improved understanding of the external industrial domain.

From this step, it is then straightforward to start the implementation of change, by identifying concrete steps to commit to for the individual, for example formulated by the technical leader him/herself as input to the personal development plan by sketching 1–3 actions aimed at taking right away, mid-term (e.g., within 3 months), or long-term (e.g., within 2 years).

## Method of Application Within the Technical Leadership Program

The method has been used as an integral part of every cohort of the Technical leadership program from the start in the following way:

![How the method is used during the program: introduce the method and tools, use the tools to define and reflect on technical leadership, and at the end use the method to prepare for the next step, with inputs to individual development plans and how to use the tools in a company context](/technicalleader3-program.png)

After introducing the tools for the participants in the early start of the program, they are used to reason about technical leadership. In this phase, participants are encouraged to challenge the content to ensure that the tools become meaningful for them in their situation. The participants are then applying the method to their individual context during the program. At the end, they use the method to prepare for the next steps after the program – how they want to change and grow in their technical leadership roles in the future, as well as how the tools can be used in a local context, specific for each company.

## More About the Technical Leadership Program

The creation of the Technical leadership program is funded by the Swedish research funding agency Vinnova, through its [Advanced Digitalisation program](https://www.avanceraddigitalisering.se/en/) and the initiative [Expert Learning Lab](https://www.avanceraddigitalisering.se/expert-learning-lab-ett-banbrytande-initiativ-for-att-starka-sveriges-kompetens-inom-avancerad-digitalisering/).

The program has been co-created with the participating Swedish engineering companies ABB, Ericsson, Saab and Volvo Group, in learning collaboration with the four Swedish research institutes and universities Chalmers University of Technology, Linköping University, Royal Institute of Technology, and Örebro University. It is an advanced program for senior technical leaders within these engineering companies, with the aim to increase their understanding of their technical leader role to become the technical leaders their companies need to succeed today and in the future.

The Technical leadership program creates a valuable and effective scene for sharing experiences and boosting each other across the engineering disciplines and product areas from electronics and energy systems, communications, trucks and heavy vehicles to defense platform products. In the program, the participants explore what it means to be a technical leader and get to practice many of the needed skills, such as communication, creating strategies and design thinking. This is done with a mix of physical meetings, online workshops, individual homework and technical challenges to be solved in mixed groups where the new tools and cross-company thinking is encouraged in a completely new technical or product setting.

Within this context, the participants also get the chance to learn from each other. One very valuable insight is that, although they come from different technical and business contexts, the technical leadership challenges they face are similar.
