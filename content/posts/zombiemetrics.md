Someone in the meeting has an idea: wouldn't it be good if we measured X? Management agrees. It gets implemented. Nobody acts on it.

A zombie metric is tracked, it's visible, it changes — and nothing happens when it moves. No owner. No defined threshold that triggers action. It was added when someone had an idea, and survived because removing it felt risky. But you spend time and maintenance on something without value. And they multiply, it is easy to add more, it looks good on the dashboards. 

Test your metrics. For each metric: who owns it, and what do they do when it crosses a threshold? If you can't answer both, it's a zombie. 

And how do you get rid of the bad ones? Put an expiry date on new metrics when you add them. Force yourself to decide that you have a good reason to keep it. The best metric is one you're willing to delete when it is no longer needed.
