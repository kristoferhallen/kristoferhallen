Building internal products is a somewhat different challenge compared to a commercial product. Unlike consumer apps chasing  growth, you're serving your colleagues. But that doesn't mean measurement matters less. If anything, it matters more, because every dollar spent on internal tooling needs to justify itself against other company priorities.

What should I as a product manager measure to know if I am doing the right thing?

# From the User Perspective
* Adoption and engagement tell you if people find your product valuable. Track active users, feature usage, and track who isn't using your product. 

* Time saved. This metric translates directly to business value.

* User satisfaction through regular surveys or feedback sessions keeps you honest. 

# From the Company Perspective
* Cost per user helps you understand efficiency. You can't only look at this measurement, there can be tools that are critical for the company, expensive and still only used by a few people or the usage could be indirect through automation. 
* ROI and payback period answer the question every executive asks: "Was this worth it?" 
* Support burden reveals product quality. 
* Opportunity cost is harder to measure but critical to consider. Every engineer on your team could be working on revenue-generating features or other internal tools. Regularly ask: is this still the highest-value problem we could be solving?

# The Balance
A good internal product managers balances both sides of this equation. Users who love a product that costs too much won't save it from budget cuts. A cheap product that nobody uses is just wasted money.
