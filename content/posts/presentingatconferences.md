I used to think conference talks were mainly about giving back to the community or building my own and the company's brand. But I also realized that the process of creating a talk for an external audience forces you to understand your own company better.
You decide to present on something your team has been working on. You think you know the topic and then you start building the talk. To make your content valuable for an external audience, you can't just describe what you do. You need to understand why you do it, what problems it solved, and what the actual results were. So you start digging, collect data and reflect on how it works for you. Why did we choose to do it this way? How has it worked out for us?

The business case
From a business perspective, this is a high-ROI activity. You're  getting a free internal audit and knowledge synthesis exercise, packaged as an external engagement.

It doesn't stop there. Once you give the talk and get questions from the audience, you gain even more perspective. People will ask about edge cases you didn't consider, share how they solved similar problems differently, or point out implications you missed. So next time someone suggests your team present at a conference, don't just think about the external benefits. Think about what you'll learn about your own company in the process.
