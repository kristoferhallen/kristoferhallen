![Modern software engineering book cover](/modernsoftwareengineering.jpg#floatleft) Modern software engineering written by David Farley is a good book to read if you want an overview of a systematic approach to software development that has focus on real delivering real impact instead of where we many times end up planning and focusing on being busy. It is not about throwing away process. It is about a systematic approach (this is engineering) and making use of what makes software different from many other areas, we can create, run, learn from and change the software in minutes. A short summary would be - waterfall is not a good practice in software development, make use of the superpower of short experiments. The book adds on a lot to this, measure, test, characteristics of good software and also some perspectives on the people and organisation that works with the software. 
    
Reading this and reflecting on my organization I got the insight that iterative development is not enough. There is a trap, you continue with the waterfall approach, just split in smaller pieces. You still make a long plan, you don´t learn and change. You have to go the full cycle, create, run, learn, change (plans, implementation…) based on learning.
