Ask Your Developer: How to Harness the Power of Software Developers and Win in the 21st Century by Jeff Lawson was the book that influenced me the most last year. It is about how to run a software development company and the main theme is what the title says, use your developers competence and problems solving competence. Give them problems that you want them to solve instead of  detailed descriptions of what to do. This does not mean that everyone does whatever they want, the company needs to set the direction, the focus and the guardrails. My main takeaways:

* When you set up a team you must know the team mission, the team customer (can be internal) and how to measure progress (that is in line with the mission).
* When the workload gets to big for a team split it. Prepare the split, follow the three rules above.
* Don't focus on better collaboration, instead reduce the need for collaboration with well defined interfaces.
* If you can try things in a low risk way and quickly learn about customer needs why shouldn't you? And in most cases you can do that in software development. Experiment, start small, scale up and quickly kill things that don't work.

The rules for setting up teams is something I have been thinking a lot about and I find it hard to find good measurements. It might be connected with the other two rules, if you don't have a clear mission and customer the measurements will be hard.
